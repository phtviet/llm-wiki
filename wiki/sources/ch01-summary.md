# AIE — AI Engineering (Chip Huyen, O'Reilly, 2024)

Immutable source. Citation label **AIE**. Cite as (AIE p.NNN) using printed book pages.
Covered by this ingest: Chapter 1 closing summary, book pp.47-48.

This section closes Chapter 1. It notes growing JavaScript-ecosystem support for AI
tooling (LangChain.js, Transformers.js, OpenAI's Node library, Vercel's AI SDK), and
observes that AI engineers increasingly come from full-stack/web backgrounds rather
than traditional ML backgrounds, giving them an edge at quickly turning ideas into
demos (AIE p.47). It contrasts the traditional ML engineering workflow -- data first,
model training, then product last -- with the new AI engineering workflow enabled by
readily available foundation models, which allows starting with a product and only
investing in data and models once the product shows promise; in traditional ML
engineering, model development and product development are often disjointed, whereas
AI engineers tend to be much more involved in building the product (AIE p.47).

The chapter summary then recaps the chapter's arc: the transition from language models
to large language models via self-supervision, the incorporation of other data
modalities to form foundation models, the rise of AI engineering, notable application
patterns for consumers and enterprises, the question of whether to build an
application at all, and the AI engineering stack and how it differs from ML
engineering (AIE p.47-48). It closes by noting AI's use for information aggregation as
a way to keep up with a fast-moving field (AIE p.48).
