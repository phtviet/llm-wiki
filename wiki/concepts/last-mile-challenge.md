# Last-Mile Challenge

The last-mile challenge is the observation that progressing from an initial working demo to a finished, reliable AI product becomes disproportionately harder as the product nears completion. Foundation models' impressive base capabilities make early demos easy to build, but a good demo does not promise a good end product (AIE p.33). Ding et al. (2023), in the UltraChat paper, described it as: the journey from 0 to 60 is easy, whereas progressing from 60 to 100 becomes exceedingly challenging (AIE p.33).

LinkedIn (2024) reported the same pattern in production: reaching 80% of their desired experience took one month, but that early success led them to underestimate the remaining work -- it took four more months to surpass 95%, most of it spent fixing product kinks and hallucinations, with each subsequent 1% gain progressively slower and more discouraging to achieve (AIE p.33-34).
