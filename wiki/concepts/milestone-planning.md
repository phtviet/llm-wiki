# Milestone Planning

Milestone planning is the process of laying out a plan to reach a product's measurable goals once a [[usefulness-threshold]] has been set. Where to start depends on evaluating existing off-the-shelf models to understand their current capabilities: the stronger those models already are at the task, the less additional work is required. For example, if the goal is to automate 60% of customer support tickets and an off-the-shelf model already automates 30%, the remaining effort is smaller than if it automates none (AIE p.33).

Evaluation often changes the goals themselves: a team may discover after evaluating existing models that the resources needed to reach the usefulness threshold exceed the product's potential return, leading it to abandon the goal (AIE p.33).

Planning must also account for the **last mile challenge**: because foundation models already have impressive base capabilities, an initial demo can be built quickly, but a good demo does not guarantee a good end product — turning a demo into a product can take months or years even though the demo itself took a weekend (AIE p.33). The UltraChat paper (Ding et al., 2023) is quoted describing this as the journey from 0 to 60 being easy while 60 to 100 becomes exceedingly challenging. LinkedIn (2024) reported a matching experience: reaching 80% of their desired experience took one month, which led them to grossly underestimate how much further work remained (AIE p.33).

## Key figures
- Example goal: automate 60% of customer support tickets, against an off-the-shelf baseline of 30% (AIE p.33)
- LinkedIn: one month to reach 80% of the desired experience (AIE p.33)
