# AI Writing

Writing is one of the most established use cases for foundation models: autocorrect and auto-completion on smartphones have long relied on it, and it suits AI well because writing is frequent, tedious, and tolerant of mistakes -- a bad suggestion can simply be ignored (AIE p.22). LLMs are especially suited to writing because they are trained for text completion (AIE p.23).

Use cases span consumers, creators, and enterprises. Consumers use AI to rewrite tone (e.g. turning an angry email pleasant) or expand bullet points into full paragraphs. Students use it to write essays, and some writers use it to write entire books, including startups producing children's, fan fiction, romance, and fantasy books; AI-generated books can be interactive, changing plot based on reader preference. Note-taking and email apps such as Google Docs, Notion, and Gmail build in AI writing assistance (AIE p.23). Enterprises use AI writing for sales, marketing, performance reports, cold outreach, ad copywriting, and product descriptions, including through CRM tools like HubSpot and Salesforce (AIE p.23).

AI writing can also be abused: in 2023 the New York Times reported Amazon was flooded with shoddy AI-generated travel guidebooks complete with fabricated author bios, websites, and reviews (AIE p.23).

An MIT study on ChatGPT's impact on writing measured productivity and adoption effects among professionals (AIE p.23).

## Key figures
- MIT study assigned occupation-specific writing tasks to 453 college-educated professionals, randomly exposing half to ChatGPT (AIE p.23)
- Among those exposed to ChatGPT, average time taken decreased by 40% and output quality rose by 18% (AIE p.23)
- Workers exposed to ChatGPT were 2 times as likely to report using it in their real job two weeks after the experiment, and 1.6 times as likely two months after (AIE p.23)
