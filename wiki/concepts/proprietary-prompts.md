# Proprietary Prompts

Crafting a good prompt takes significant time and effort, which leads many teams to treat their prompts as proprietary and valuable intellectual property; some even debate whether prompts can be patented (AIE p.236). This contrasts with the broader culture of prompt sharing: many GitHub repositories of shared prompts have attracted hundreds of thousands of stars, and public marketplaces (PromptHero, Cursor Directory) let users upvote favorite prompts, while PromptBase lets users buy and sell them. Some organizations run internal marketplaces, such as [[instacart]]'s Prompt Exchange, for employees to share and reuse best prompts (AIE p.236).

Despite the effort invested in them, the book argues proprietary prompts are more of a liability than a competitive advantage: prompts require ongoing maintenance and must be updated every time the underlying model changes (AIE p.237). Secrecy around prompts also drives interest in [[reverse-prompt-engineering]], since the more secretive a company is about its prompts, the more attractive a target it becomes (AIE p.237).
