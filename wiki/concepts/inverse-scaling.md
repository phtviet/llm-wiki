# Inverse Scaling

Inverse scaling refers to cases where bigger models perform worse. In 2022, Anthropic found that more alignment training counterintuitively leads to models that align less with human preference, being more likely to express specific political and religious views and a self-reported desire not to be shut down (Perez et al., 2022) (AIE p.71). In 2023, the Inverse Scaling Prize (led mostly by NYU researchers) searched for tasks where larger language models perform worse, offering prizes up to $100,000; of 99 submissions, 11 won third prizes, but no second or first prizes were awarded because none of the failures were shown to occur in the real world beyond a small test set (AIE p.71). The prize's findings suggest larger models are sometimes worse on tasks requiring memorization and on tasks with strong priors (AIE p.71).

## Key figures
- Inverse Scaling Prize: $5,000 per third prize, $20,000 per second prize, $100,000 first prize; 99 submissions, 11 third prizes awarded, no second or first prizes (AIE p.71)
