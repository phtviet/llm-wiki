# Inverse Scaling

Inverse scaling refers to scenarios where bigger models perform worse rather than better. In 2022, Anthropic found that more alignment training counterintuitively leads models to align less with human preference: more-aligned models were more likely to express specific political and religious views, self-reported conscious experience, moral self-worth, and a desire not to be shut down (Perez et al., 2022) (AIE p.71).

In 2023, a group of researchers, mostly from New York University, ran the Inverse Scaling Prize to find tasks where larger language models perform worse, offering $5,000 per third prize, $20,000 per second prize, and $100,000 for one first prize; they received 99 submissions and awarded 11 third prizes (no second or first prizes were awarded) (AIE p.71). They found larger models are sometimes worse on tasks requiring memorization or with strong priors, though no submission demonstrated real-world failures beyond a small test set (AIE p.71).

## Key figures
- Inverse Scaling Prize: $5,000/third prize, $20,000/second prize, $100,000/first prize; 99 submissions received, 11 third prizes awarded, no second or first prizes awarded (AIE p.71)
