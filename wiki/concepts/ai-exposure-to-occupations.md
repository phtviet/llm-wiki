# AI Exposure to Occupations

AI exposure is a measure, defined by Eloundou et al. (2023), of how much AI and AI-powered software can accelerate the tasks of a given occupation. A task is 'exposed' if AI can reduce the time needed to complete it by at least 50%; an occupation's exposure percentage is the share of its tasks that are exposed (AIE p.17). The study distinguishes exposure to AI models directly (labeled alpha) from exposure to AI-powered software (labeled beta and zeta).

Occupations with 100% or near-100% exposure include interpreters and translators, tax preparers, web designers, and writers; humans labeled 15 occupations as 'fully exposed'. Occupations with no exposure include cooks, stonemasons, and athletes (AIE p.17). The study is used in the book as evidence of which categories of work generative AI use cases are best suited for.

## Key figures
- Exposure defined as at least 50% reduction in time needed for a task (AIE p.17)
- 15 occupations labeled 'fully exposed' (100% exposure), including interpreters/translators, tax preparers, financial quantitative analysts, writers and authors, and web/digital interface designers (AIE p.17)
- Survey researchers: 76.5% (human alpha), 84.4% (human beta) exposure (AIE p.17)
