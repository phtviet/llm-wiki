# Conversational Bots

Conversational bots are AI applications that interact with users through dialogue. They can help people find information, explain concepts, brainstorm ideas, and act as companions or therapists, including emulating specific personalities so a user can talk to a digital copy of someone (AIE p.26).

Researchers have also used groups of conversational bots to simulate a society, enabling studies of social dynamics (Park et al., 2023) (AIE p.26).

For enterprises, the most common form is the customer support bot, which can lower costs and respond to users faster than human agents. AI can also serve as a product copilot, guiding customers through complex tasks such as filing insurance claims, doing taxes, or looking up corporate policies (AIE p.26).

Text is not the only interface for conversational agents: voice assistants such as Google Assistant, Siri, and Alexa predate the recent wave of text-based bots, and 3D conversational bots are already used in games and are gaining traction in retail and marketing. One use case is AI-powered non-player characters (NPCs), which are traditionally scripted with a limited range of dialogue but can be made much smarter with AI, potentially changing the dynamics of existing games like The Sims and Skyrim and enabling new kinds of games (AIE p.26).
