# Competitive Advantage (Technology, Data, Distribution)

In AI, competitive advantage generally comes from three sources: technology, data, and
distribution (the ability to bring a product in front of users). With foundation models,
the core technology used by most companies tends to be similar, so it does not
reliably differentiate. Distribution advantage tends to belong to big, established
companies. The data advantage is more nuanced: big companies often have more
existing data, but a startup that reaches market first and accumulates enough usage
data can turn data into its moat, continually improving its product. Even where user
data cannot be used to train models directly, usage information still yields insight into
user behavior and product shortcomings, which can guide data collection and training
(AIE p.32).

A related risk this framework explains: many startups build products that could
instead be a feature of an existing large product (e.g., a feature Google Docs or
Microsoft Office could add), leaving them exposed if a big company decides to
replicate the feature quickly. Despite this, many successful companies began as a
feature-sized idea that larger competitors had overlooked (AIE p.32).
