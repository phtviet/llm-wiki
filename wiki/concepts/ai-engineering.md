# AI Engineering

AI engineering is the process of building applications on top of existing foundation models, as distinct from traditional ML engineering (MLOps), which involves developing ML models from scratch (AIE p.12). Three factors together create ideal conditions for its rapid growth: general-purpose AI capabilities that expand the range of possible applications, increased AI investment lowering the cost and raising the returns of building AI products, and a low entrance barrier from the model-as-a-service API approach and AI-assisted coding, which let people build applications with minimal software engineering background (AIE p.12-14).

The author chose the term 'AI engineering' over alternatives (ML engineering, MLOps, AIOps, LLMOps) because ML engineering does not capture how foundation-model work differs from traditional ML work, and the 'Ops' terms understate the emphasis on tweaking (engineering) foundation models rather than operating them; a survey of 20 practitioners also favored the term (AIE p.15).

## Key figures
- Goldman Sachs Research estimated AI investment could approach $100 billion in the US and $200 billion globally by 2025 (AIE p.13)
- One in three S&P 500 companies mentioned AI in Q2 2023 earnings calls, three times the rate a year earlier (AIE p.13)
- Companies mentioning AI in earnings calls saw an average 4.6% stock price increase vs. 2.4% for those that did not (AIE p.13-14)
- Within two years, four open source AI engineering tools (AutoGPT, Stable Diffusion web UI, LangChain, Ollama) garnered more GitHub stars than Bitcoin, and are on track to surpass [[react-framework|React]] and Vue (AIE p.14)
- Terms like 'Generative AI,' 'ChatGPT,' '[[prompt-engineering]],' and 'Prompt Crafting' were added to LinkedIn profiles at an average 75% month-over-month increase as of August 2023 (AIE p.14)
