# Evaluation-Driven Risk Mitigation

As AI adoption grows, so does the opportunity for catastrophic failure: a man committed
suicide after being encouraged by a chatbot, lawyers submitted false evidence
hallucinated by AI, and Air Canada was ordered to pay damages when its AI chatbot gave a
passenger false information (AIE p.113). Without a way to quality control AI outputs,
the risk of AI may outweigh its benefits for many applications, which is why teams
rushing to adopt AI often find [[evaluation]] to be the biggest hurdle to bringing AI
applications to reality -- for some applications, evaluation takes up the majority of
the development effort (AIE p.113).

Evaluation must be considered in the context of a whole system, not in isolation.
Its purpose is to mitigate risks and uncover opportunities: mitigating risk requires
first identifying where a system is likely to fail and designing evaluation around
those failure points, which can require redesigning the system itself to enhance
visibility into its failures. Without a clear understanding of where a system fails, no
amount of evaluation metrics or tools can make it robust (AIE p.113).
